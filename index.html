Family Nursing Process
in Public Health

“Systematic, dynamic, interpersonal process — nurse and family as partners.”

Foundations: Family & Nursing Process

The family is the basic unit of the community. In public health nursing, the family is viewed as a system; health of members is interdependent. The Community Health Nursing Process is a systematic, scientific, dynamic, ongoing interpersonal process in which nurses and clients (family) are viewed as a system affecting each other.

Goal: Achieve optimum level of functioning (OLOF) for the family.
Key principle: Partnership, empowerment, and active participation of family members.

Phase 1: Comprehensive Family Assessment

Data collection through home visit, interview, observation, physical exam, review of records. Categories of data:

  • Family structure & demographics (genogram, ecomap)
  • Socio-economic & cultural factors
  • Environmental conditions (housing, water, sanitation)
  • Health status of each member (morbidity, nutrition, immunization)
  • Family coping patterns & health behaviors
Health Problem Categories (NLPGN): Health deficit (gap in health status, e.g., TB), Health threat (conditions that promote disease, e.g., unsafe water), Foreseeable crisis (anticipated stress, e.g., unemployment).

Phase 2: Family Nursing Diagnosis

After data analysis, the PHN formulates a diagnostic statement that reflects the family's response to health problems and factors maintaining them. Diagnosis includes:

  • Statement of unhealthful response or negative condition
  • Factors that maintain or prevent change
  • Family’s capabilities and limitations

Example: “Inability to provide adequate nutrition related to poverty and lack of knowledge on balanced diet, as evidenced by underweight children and consumption of high-fat, low-vegetable meals.”

Validation with family members is essential.

Phase 3: Family Care Planning

Prioritization criteria: Nature of problem (deficit vs threat), modifiability, preventive potential, and salience (family's perception).

SMART objectives (Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Realistic, Time-bound) are set with the family.

Components of care plan: goals, nursing interventions (health teaching, home nursing, referral), timeline, evaluation parameters.

Phase 4: Implementation — Family-Centered Interventions

  • Direct nursing care: wound care, BP monitoring, postnatal care, medication administration using DOH protocols.
  • Health teaching & counseling: nutrition, breastfeeding, family planning, hygiene, disease prevention.
  • Home visit & bag technique: essential public health nursing activity to provide care in the family’s environment.
  • Coordination & referral: linking families to midwives, BHWs, physicians, social welfare, PhilHealth.
  • Empowerment: teach family members to perform health tasks independently.

Phase 5: Evaluation of Family Outcomes

Evaluates structure, process, and outcome. Focus on improvement in family’s capability to perform health tasks:

  • Recognizing health problems
  • Seeking appropriate care
  • Managing health and non-health crises
  • Providing nursing care to sick/dependent members
  • Maintaining a healthful environment
  • Utilizing community resources effectively

Outcome indicators: change in knowledge, practice, morbidity/mortality, family satisfaction. Evaluation results used to revise diagnosis and plan.

Family Health Tasks (Maglaya)

First identified by Freeman and adapted by Maglaya, these tasks determine the family’s ability to address health problems:

  1. Recognizing the presence of a health problem or interruption of development.
  2. Making decisions about seeking appropriate health care.
  3. Providing nursing care to the sick, disabled, or dependent member.
  4. Maintaining a home environment conducive to good health and personal development.
  5. Utilizing community resources for health care.

These tasks are used as evaluation criteria for family nursing interventions.

PHN Role & Essential Tools

Functions: management, supervision, nursing care, collaboration, health promotion/education, research. Core tools:

  • Family Folder / Family Record: Contains demographic data, health history, nursing care plan.
  • Home Visit Record: Documents date, purpose, findings, interventions, health teachings, follow-up.
  • Target Client List (TCL): For EPI, prenatal, TB DOTS, family planning monitoring.
  • Genogram & Ecomap: Visual family structure and support systems.
  • Bag technique: Promotes aseptic, efficient home nursing care.
Standards of PHN (NLPGN, 2006): emphasizes nursing process, interdisciplinary collaboration, and ethical accountability.
